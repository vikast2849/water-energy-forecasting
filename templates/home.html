The Urban-Rural Resource Divide

Traditional municipal forecasting models treat urban metropolises and rural farming blocks identically. This flawed assumption leads to:

  • Urban Inefficiencies: Excessive water allocation and peak-hour electricity grid stress in high-density industrial hubs.
  • Rural Vulnerabilities: Acute water deficits and uncoordinated 3-phase agricultural power rationing during dry summer crop cycles.
  • Inflexible Models: Failure to account for localized non-linear climate triggers (droughts, seasonal migration).

Framework Core Objectives

Our machine learning architecture directly resolves these challenges through four primary objectives:

  • Demographic & Climate Integration: Synthesize population density, household sizes, temperature, and rainfall into standardized ML feature spaces.
  • Tri-Model Execution: Deploy SVM for non-linear continuous regression, KNN for localized rural clustering, and Logistic Regression for scarcity alert detection.
  • Decision Support: Deliver actionable dispatch schedules for water utility boards (KUWSDB) and energy distribution companies (BESCOM).

Support Vector Machine

SVR / SVC

Projects input attributes into high-dimensional space via an RBF kernel. Ideal for modeling non-linear urban water and electrical consumption across temperature gradients.

Variance Explained: 99.86% R²

K-Nearest Neighbors

KNN (k=5)

Applies Euclidean distance-weighted interpolation to nearest geographic & demographic archetypes. Excelled in rural agrarian taluks like Challakere and Molakalmuru.

Variance Explained: 98.85% R²

Logistic Regression

LogReg

Translates multi-factor stresses into calibrated sigmoid probabilities of scarcity risk. Provides transparent log-odds feature importance coefficients for utility planning.

Classification Accuracy: 91.35%
